Price, spread and depth

Kalshi returns bids only. Arrays are ascending, so the best bid is the last element. The implied YES ask is 1.00 − best NO bid and the YES mid is the average of the two. Contract depth is the sum of count_fp at every resting price level, reported as contracts available at each price.

Swing threshold

An alert is raised when the YES mid moves by at least $0.10 between two consecutive observations of the same market. Missing quotes are skipped, never treated as zero. A $0.10 move on a $1 contract is 10 cents of settlement value.

The all-events chat adds a second, clearly labelled basis: moves of at least the same threshold measured between two consecutive closes of the official candlestick series Kalshi publishes for the market. Kalshi returns no two-sided book for many demo markets, so the midpoint rule correctly fires nothing on them while the exchange's own published price history still shows a ≥ $0.10 step; hiding that would be an omission, claiming it was tradeable would be a fabrication, so the row carries its basis and a link to the candlestick response behind both numbers. These rows never enter the model shortlist, which stays gated on an executable midpoint. An empty book — 0.0000 bid against 1.0000 ask, the documented bids-only shape of a market with nothing resting — is not used as a price anywhere on this site.

Fees

Simulated fills use Kalshi's published taker fee, ceil(M × 0.07 × C × P × (1 − P)) to the cent, from the official fee schedule. All 42 published rows of that table are reproduced exactly by the implementation and are asserted in tests/test_engine.py. The trading/ engine respects each series' own fee_multiplier; the collector's simulated ledger, which is what trades.html publishes, applies a multiplier of 1 to every series because it calls kalshi_fee(price, contracts) without one — that difference is listed on Flagged for review rather than left implicit.

The high-ROI filter

Fair value is the mid, pulled toward 0.50 in proportion to the spread — a wide book means the mid is unreliable. Edge is fair value minus the price actually paid to cross the spread. Net ROI divides edge after fees by that price. A market is shortlisted only when net ROI clears the strategy's threshold and the spread is tight. This is a screen, not a forecast; the performance table shows realised results including every loss.

Settlement

Positions settle from the market's official result field. On a Yes outcome, Yes contract holders receive $1.00 per contract; on a No outcome, No contract holders receive $1.00 per contract — the losing side pays nothing. Only net positions are settled. Source: Market Settlement.

What is deliberately not claimed

  • No news article is attributed to a price move. The alert links to the market, not to a cause we cannot verify.
  • The paper ledger is not your demo account balance. Authenticated portfolio endpoints require credentials this repo does not hold.
  • Unadjusted open interest is not traded dollar volume.
